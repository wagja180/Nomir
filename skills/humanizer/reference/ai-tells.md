# Common AI-Writing Tells

Scan a draft for these before deciding it's ready. Each one is individually normal in small doses — the tell is several of them stacking up in one short piece of text.

## Phrasing tells
- Stock openers: "In today's world," "In this day and age," "Let's dive in," "Without further ado."
- Throat-clearing: "It's important to note that," "It's worth mentioning," "At the end of the day."
- Buzzword inflation: "game changer," "elevate," "unlock," "embark on a journey," "navigate the landscape," "unleash."
- Over-hedging: "might potentially," "could possibly," "in many cases, it may be true that" — says nothing concrete.
- False balance on a short caption: "On one hand... on the other hand..." where no real tension exists.
- Formal connectors in casual content: "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally" — fine in an essay, off-tone in a Reel caption.

## Structural tells
- Perfectly symmetric rule-of-three lists where each item is generic filler ("faster, better, stronger" with nothing specific behind any word).
- Every sentence roughly the same length — real speech and real captions vary rhythm.
- Em dash used as the default punctuation for every pause, instead of periods, commas, or just a line break.
- No contractions anywhere ("do not" instead of "don't," "it is" instead of "it's") in content that's meant to sound spoken.
- Title-case or heavy bolding applied to phrases that don't need emphasis.
- Emoji used as a generic bullet in front of every line rather than to carry actual meaning.

## Content tells
- No concrete, idiosyncratic detail — a real person's story has one weird specific (a number, a place, a thing that went wrong) that a generic version wouldn't include.
- Exclamation points doing the work that specificity should be doing.
- A conclusion that restates the opening instead of adding something new.

## What "humanized" looks like instead
- Varied sentence length, including a few short fragments.
- At least one concrete, specific, slightly odd detail that couldn't apply to just anyone.
- Contractions, and punctuation that matches how it'd actually be said out loud.
- Cut hedges — say the thing directly, or don't say it.
- A caption that could not be copy-pasted onto a different niche account without editing — specificity is the test.
